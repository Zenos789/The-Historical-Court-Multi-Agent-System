Genghis Khan, the founder and first khan of the Mongol Empire, is recognized for successfully uniting the Mongol tribes and establishing an integrated meritocracy from their tribal structure. He led extensive military campaigns, conquering vast territories across China and Central Asia, which facilitated significant commercial and cultural exchange. He is also regarded as the founding father of the Mongolian nation.

However, his reign was marked by widespread devastation and atrocities. During the Mongol invasion of the Khwarazmian Empire (1219-1221), there was immense destruction. Under his command, his son Tolui carried out campaigns with "ruthless efficiency," leading to massacres in cities like Nishapur and Merv, where medieval chroniclers attributed millions of deaths, highlighting the abnormal brutality of the Mongol conquests. Genghis Khan was known for his ruthlessness towards enemies, and the Mongol army under his command is associated with the deaths of millions. He is remembered in some regions, such as Russia and the Arab world, as a backwards, savage tyrant.
